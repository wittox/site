What it
cleans up.

The clutter that piles up on every phone, found for you, and cleared only when you say so.

01

Duplicate photos

Exact copies, and the same picture saved smaller or recompressed by a chat app. Then the near-identical ones — bursts and retakes — grouped for you to look at, with a suggestion of which one to keep.

02

Clean with AI

Write what you want gone: buildings, cats, receipts, food, cars, sunsets. “Or” finds either, “and” finds both in the same photo. The AI runs on the phone and marks the photos it is unsure about, so you decide.

03

Duplicate contacts

The same person saved more than once, matched by number, email or name, then merged the way Android's own Contacts app does: every account keeps its data and keeps syncing, and “Separate” undoes it.

04

Numbers to tidy

Numbers saved without the country prefix get it, so WhatsApp and the duplicate search recognise them; calls work as before. The same number repeated inside one contact is removed.

05

Screenshots and videos

The same screen captured twice, screenshots older than a month, videos saved more than once, and the biggest ones — which it can also shrink to at most 1080p.

06

The rest of the clutter

Blurred photos, black shots taken in a pocket, memes piling up from WhatsApp and Telegram. Or swipe through the gallery: left to delete, right to keep.

How it works

It finds.
You decide.

01

Open a section

Photos, screenshots, videos, contacts or AI. Each permission is asked for only when you open the section that needs it, never at launch.

02

It looks, once

Every picture gets a small fingerprint, and the AI learns what is in your photos the first time only. Both are kept on the phone, so the next scan looks at new photos alone.

03

You choose

Results come grouped, with a suggestion of what to keep. Compare, change the selection, hide a group that is not really a duplicate. Nothing is touched yet.

04

Android confirms

Photos and videos go to Android's own trash, after Android asks you, and stay recoverable for 30 days. Before any change to your contacts a .vcf backup is saved in Download.

Where the work happens

On the phone, and nowhere else. Clean ’Em All does not declare Android's internet permission, so the system will not let it connect to anything — not for the duplicate search, not for the AI. Your photos and your address book never leave the device, and there is no server for them to go to.

FAQ

Questions
worth asking.

Are my photos or contacts uploaded anywhere?+

No. The app does not have Android's internet permission, so it cannot connect to anything. The duplicate search and the AI both run on the phone. The full details are in the privacy policy.

What can the AI find?+

Write what you want to clean out — “buildings”, “cats or dogs”, “receipts”, “food”, “cars”, “sunsets”. “Or” finds either, “and” finds both in the same photo. Photos it is unsure about are marked “maybe?” for you to tick yourself, and if it does not know a word it tells you which ones it understands. The first time, it goes through the gallery once; after that it is quick.

Can I get back something I deleted?+

Yes. Photos and videos go to Android's own trash, only after Android asks you to confirm, and stay recoverable from the Gallery app for 30 days. Contacts are backed up to a .vcf file in Download before every bulk change, and a merge can be undone with “Separate”.

What does merging contacts actually do?+

The same as Android's Contacts app: the duplicate entries are linked into one contact, nothing is deleted, and every account — Google, SIM, phone — keeps its own data and keeps syncing.

What does “numbers to tidy” change?+

Numbers saved without the country prefix get it added, so WhatsApp and the duplicate search recognise them; calling them works exactly as before. A number repeated inside the same contact is removed. A backup is saved first.

Which phones does it run on?+

Android 11 and newer. The interface is in English, Italian, Spanish, French, German and Portuguese, and follows the phone's language.
