Honesty first

What it is,
and what it isn't.

A satellite photo is a wonderful thing. It is also not a selfie, and the app never pretends otherwise.

You won't see yourself

Each pixel covers 10 × 10 metres, so nobody can be made out in the photo. But it really is the image taken from space at that moment, above you.

The satellite doesn't see you wave

The greeting is recorded by your phone. The satellite sees neither you nor the gesture; your wave is matched to the time of the shot.

“Scheduled” until it is real

A pass stays “scheduled” until the scene shows up in the catalogue. Sometimes the satellite does not acquire, and the app tells you so.

Clouds happen

If you were under a cloud, the app tells you. With automatic photos on, a cloudy pass saves only a note, so no data is spent on a white square.

FAQ

Questions
worth asking.

Will I be in the photo?+

Not visibly. One pixel covers 10 × 10 metres, so people cannot be made out. What you get is the genuine image taken from space at the moment of the pass, above the place where you were, with a circle marking the spot.

Does the satellite see me wave?+

No. Your phone records the wave and its exact time, and the app tells you how many seconds before or after the shot it happened. The satellite takes its picture regardless.

How long until the photo arrives?+

It depends on when ESA publishes the scene, usually within a few hours. Until then the pass is shown as “scheduled”. If the satellite did not acquire that day, or you were under a cloud, the app tells you.

Which satellites?+

The Sentinel-2 satellites of the European Copernicus programme, operated by ESA. The images come through Earth Search by Element 84, and the orbits from CelesTrak.

Does it need my location?+

It works best with it, but your exact position stays on the phone and is used only while the app is open. Images are searched over a wider, rounded area. If you prefer, choose a city from a list instead.

Which phones does it run on?+

Android 8 and newer. The interface is in Italian, English, Spanish, French, German and Portuguese.
