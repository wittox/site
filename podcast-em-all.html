A reading, or a conversation

Pick a faithful single-voice reading of the source, or a dialogue where two hosts discuss it. Same document, two very different listens.

The voice is made on the phone

Speech is synthesised on the device with Supertonic 3, one line at a time. No cloud text-to-speech, and no audio of you or your documents sent anywhere.

Ten voices

Five female and five male presets. A dialogue casts two of them, one per host, so the two speakers stay distinct all the way through.

As deep as you want

Choose overview, balanced or deep, and the script is written to match — a five-minute gist or a proper walk through the detail.

Keeps going without you

Generation runs in an Android foreground service, so it carries on if you close the app and picks up again after a reboot. Each finished line is saved as it is made.

Player and export

Play it back at the speed you like, and export the finished piece to MP3 to keep or share.

How it works

Four steps, and only one of them needs you.

01

Add your sources

One or several at a time: PDF, Word, plain text, Markdown, or a pasted link to an article. Pick the language you want to listen in and how deep it should go.

02

The text is pulled out and a script is written

Clean text is extracted from each source, then a language model turns it into either a reading or a two-host dialogue in the language you chose. This is the one step that uses a server.

03

Your phone does the talking

The script is synthesised on the device, line by line, and stitched into a single track. Every line is saved the moment it is made, so nothing is lost if the phone is busy, closed or restarted.

04

Listen, or take it with you

Finished pieces land in the library. Play at your own speed, or export to MP3 and put it wherever you keep your audio.

Where the work happens

Being straight about this, because the other app in this family does everything offline and this one cannot. Writing the script needs a language model, and that runs on a server: the text of your sources is sent there to be turned into a script. The audio is entirely different — speech is generated on your phone, so no recording and no synthesised voice ever leaves the device. If listening to private documents matters to you, that distinction is the one to know.

FAQ

Questions worth asking

When can I get it?

It is in closed testing and not on Google Play for everyone yet. There is no release date to promise, and it will not be announced here until it is real.

What will it cost?

Not decided yet, so there is no price on this page. What is already settled: it will not be free, and whatever the price turns out to be, it will not be an advert-supported app.

Does it work offline?

Partly, and the distinction matters. Writing the script needs a language model on a server, so that step needs a connection and sends the text of your sources. Turning the script into speech happens entirely on your phone, so the audio itself is never uploaded or streamed.

Does it sound like a robot?

It uses Supertonic 3, a current on-device speech model, at 44.1kHz. It is not a cloud voice and will not pretend to be one, but it is a long way from the flat text-to-speech built into a phone.

What happens if I close the app?

Nothing stops. Generation runs in an Android foreground service rather than in the screen you are looking at, and each finished line is written to disk as it is made. Force-close it, or restart the phone, and it carries on from where it was.

Who makes it?

Vittorio Vesco, an independent Android developer in Turin working solo under the name The Yellow Apps — the same person behind Convert 'Em All.
